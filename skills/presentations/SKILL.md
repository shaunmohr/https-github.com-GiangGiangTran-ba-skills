# Presentations Skill for BA

Presenting is not reporting. Reporting informs. Presenting persuades.

## What is Presentation Design?

**Definition**: Crafting and delivering a structured narrative that moves an audience from their current thinking to a new position — whether that's a decision, a belief, or an action.

**Why it matters**:
- Brilliant analysis buried in a bad presentation gets rejected
- Executives form impressions in the first 90 seconds
- Q&A is where credibility is won or lost — not the slides
- The presenter's confidence in delivery affects how the content is received

**When to Use**:
- ✅ Presenting business case recommendations to leadership
- ✅ Discovery readouts to stakeholders
- ✅ Project status presentations
- ✅ Workshop facilitation and kickoffs
- ✅ Client or partner presentations

## The Presentation Structure

### **The SCR Framework (Situation → Complication → Resolution)**

```
SITUATION (15–20% of time):
"Here's the context we all share..."
- Establishes common ground with audience
- States what is true today (they should nod along)
- Sets up why the next section matters

COMPLICATION (20–25% of time):
"Here's the tension / problem / opportunity..."
- Introduces what has changed or what gap exists
- Creates the question the audience should be asking: "So what do we do?"
- This is where engagement peaks — don't rush it

RESOLUTION (55–65% of time):
"Here's what we recommend and why..."
- Your recommendation, options analysis, evidence
- Investment/risk/return
- Ask and next steps

CLOSE:
"Here's what I need from you today..."
- Explicit ask (approve, fund, decide, commit)
- Next steps with owners and dates
```

## Audience-Specific Presentation Styles

### **Executive Audience**
- Lead with conclusion (they hate buildup)
- Time: 15–20 min with Q&A built in
- Slides: 6–10 max, executive summary on slide 2
- What they want: Decision, options, risk, ask
- What to avoid: Details, methodology, qualifications
- Anticipate: "What does this cost?" "What's the risk?" "Who owns it?"

### **Technical Audience**
- Show methodology — they'll question it if you don't
- Time: 30–60 min is acceptable
- Slides: More detail OK, but still one insight per slide
- What they want: How it works, what could go wrong, trade-offs
- What to avoid: Glossing over complexity, overpromising
- Anticipate: "Why this approach?" "What are the edge cases?" "How does it scale?"

### **Stakeholder Group (Mixed)**
- Modular structure: summary for executives + detail for implementers
- Use the parking lot for deep-dive questions
- Time: 45–60 min
- What they want: Impact on them, timeline, what's expected of them
- Anticipate: "How does this affect my team?" "Who decides?"

### **Client / External**
- Extra polish and rehearsal — no internal jargon
- Lead with their problem, not your solution
- Time: Match their calendar slot (usually 30 or 60 min)
- What they want: Confidence you understand their problem
- Anticipate: "Why you?" "Show me proof." "What happens if this fails?"

## Presentation Preparation Framework

### **The PREP Process**

```
P - Purpose: What decision/action do you want at the end?
R - Research: What does your audience already know/believe?
E - Evidence: What 3 pieces of data will move them?
P - Practice: Run it once aloud before you go in
```

### **Audience Pre-work**

Before any important presentation:
1. **Identify the skeptic** — who in the room is most likely to resist?
2. **Pre-sell the decision** — brief 1:1 with key decision-makers before the meeting
3. **Know the politics** — who has influence over whom?
4. **Prepare for "no"** — what's your response if the answer is "not yet"?

## Opening Strong (First 90 Seconds)

The audience decides whether to trust you in the opening.

### **Opening Patterns That Work**:

**Pattern 1: The Provocative Fact**
"Every week, our team spends 18 hours correcting data errors that shouldn't exist. By the end of today, I want your approval to make that zero."

**Pattern 2: The Question**
"How many of you have had a project delayed because of a decision that could have been made two weeks earlier? [Pause.] That's the problem we're here to fix."

**Pattern 3: The Stakes Frame**
"In the next 20 minutes, I'll show you why the window to act on this closes in 60 days — and what we need to do before then."

### **Opening Patterns That Fail**:
- ❌ "Hi everyone, thanks for joining. So, um, today I'm going to talk about..."
- ❌ "I know you're all very busy so I'll try to be quick..."
- ❌ "Before I start, let me just say..." (delay before the point)
- ❌ Reading the agenda slide (summarise verbally then advance)

## Delivery Techniques

### **The Rule of Three Pauses**
Pause deliberately:
1. After you state the main recommendation
2. After you share a key data point
3. Before you answer a question

Silence is a tool. Most presenters fill it. Skilled presenters use it.

### **Managing the Room**

```
Signs of engagement: Eye contact, note-taking, leaning forward, questions
Signs of losing them: Checking phones, side conversations, glazed look

Recovery techniques:
- "Let me stop here — does this match what you're seeing on your end?"
- Direct question to a specific person: "[Name], what's your read on this?"
- "I'm going to skip ahead to the piece that affects your team directly..."
```

### **Handling Slides**

- Know each slide's headline before it appears (you're not reading it)
- Face the audience, not the screen
- Point to elements on your laptop screen, not by turning to the wall
- "Builds" (animations): Use only for step-by-step processes — never for decoration

## Q&A Mastery

### **The PREP Answer Framework**

```
P - Point (state your answer directly)
R - Reason (one key reason)
E - Example (one concrete example or proof point)
P - Point (restate the answer to close the loop)

Example:
Q: "Why not just use the existing system?"

P: "The existing system can't support this scale."
R: "It was built for 5,000 transactions/day. We're projecting 50,000."
E: "We hit this ceiling in the Q3 pilot — it crashed twice in week 2."
P: "So the existing system isn't a viable option beyond the short term."
```

### **Handling Hard Questions**

| Question type | Technique |
|--------------|-----------|
| You don't know the answer | "I don't have that data in front of me — I'll confirm and send today." |
| Loaded/aggressive | Separate the question from the tone: "That's an important concern. Let me address it directly..." |
| Out of scope | "That's outside today's scope — worth a separate conversation. Can we park it?" |
| Undermines your recommendation | "Here's what the data shows on that..." — return to evidence |
| Too detailed for the room | "I can walk you through the detail after the session..." |

### **Never say**:
- "Great question!" (sycophantic, signals you weren't expecting it)
- "As I mentioned earlier..." (passive-aggressive)
- "I'll be honest with you..." (implies you weren't honest before)
- "I can't answer that" (always say what you CAN do instead)

## Presentation Checklist

**Before**:
- [ ] Do you know your one-line recommendation cold?
- [ ] Slides checked for typos, formatting, correct data?
- [ ] Appendix slides ready for likely Q&A?
- [ ] Pre-reads sent (if appropriate)?
- [ ] Room/tech set up 10 minutes early?

**During**:
- [ ] Started on time?
- [ ] Checked for engagement signals at 10-min mark?
- [ ] Used pauses after key points?
- [ ] Made explicit ask at the end?

**After**:
- [ ] Summary email sent within 24 hours?
- [ ] Decisions recorded and distributed?
- [ ] Action owners confirmed?
- [ ] Commitments captured?

Send within 24 hours:

```
Subject: [Meeting Name] — Summary & Next Steps

DECISION: [What was agreed / what is still open]

[Your name]
```
