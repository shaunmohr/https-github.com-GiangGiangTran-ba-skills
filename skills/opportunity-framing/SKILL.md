# Opportunity Framing Skill for BA

The difference between a feature request and a funded initiative is how you frame it.

## What is Opportunity Framing?

**Definition**: The discipline of translating a problem, gap, or idea into a strategically-framed opportunity — with a clear problem statement, quantified impact, and compelling case for action.

**Why it matters**:
- A problem without a frame gets treated as noise
- A well-framed opportunity gets resourced and prioritized
- Executives fund solutions to problems they already feel, not problems you explain
- The frame determines who owns it, how big it is, and how urgent action is

**When to Use**:
- ✅ Taking a pain point from discovery to initiative pitch
- ✅ Responding to a leadership ask: "Is this worth doing?"
- ✅ Prioritizing a backlog of potential investments
- ✅ Building the problem statement for a business case
- ✅ Positioning a project to compete for budget

---

## The Opportunity Frame Structure

A complete opportunity frame answers 6 questions:

```
1. WHAT IS THE PROBLEM?
   Specific, evidenced, agreed-upon

2. WHO IS AFFECTED AND HOW?
   Stakeholders, customers, or operations — with scale

3. WHAT IS THE COST OF INACTION?
   What happens if we do nothing? By when?

4. WHAT IS THE PRIZE?
   The value created if we solve this well

5. WHY NOW?
   The trigger, window, or competitive pressure

6. WHY US?
   Strategic fit, capability, positioning
```

## Step 1: Define the Problem Precisely

Most initiatives fail because they solve the wrong problem.

### **Problem Statement Formula**:

```
[WHO] experiences [WHAT PROBLEM] when [CONTEXT/TRIGGER], 
which results in [MEASURABLE NEGATIVE OUTCOME].

Example:
"Operations teams experience a 4-hour manual reconciliation process 
every month-end when extracting financial data across 6 disconnected 
systems, which results in $180K/year in avoidable labour and 
a 3-day delay in reporting to the board."
```

### **Problem Scoping Test**:

Run your problem statement through these filters:

| Filter | Test |
|--------|------|
| Specific | Can you describe a real instance of this problem? |
| Evidenced | Do you have data proving it's real and significant? |
| Agreed | Would the affected stakeholders agree this is their problem? |
| Bounded | Is it clear what's inside and outside this problem? |
| Solvable | Is this within our realistic sphere of influence? |

**Warning signs of a poorly framed problem**:
- ❌ "We need a better system" (solution masquerading as problem)
- ❌ "Communication is poor" (too vague to act on)
- ❌ "The market is changing" (too broad — which market, which change, which implication?)

## Step 2: Size the Opportunity

Quantify the prize before you pitch the solution.

### **The TAM/SAM/SOM Model (Adapted for Internal BA)**

```
TOTAL OPPORTUNITY (theoretical max):
"If we completely solved this for the whole organization..."

REALISTIC OPPORTUNITY (achievable with this initiative):
"With this scope, in this timeframe, for these users..."

FIRST PHASE OPPORTUNITY (what Phase 1 delivers):
"In the first 12 months, targeting X segment..."
```

### **Value Types to Quantify**:

| Value Type | How to Measure |
|-----------|----------------|
| Time saved | Hours × Loaded labour rate |
| Error reduction | Error rate × Cost per error |
| Revenue enabled | New capability × Conversion assumption |
| Risk reduced | Probability × Impact × Mitigation factor |
| Capacity freed | FTEs × Cost, redeployable to higher-value work |
| Customer satisfaction | NPS points × Revenue per NPS point (Bain methodology) |
| Compliance value | Cost of non-compliance × Probability avoided |

### **Sizing Worksheet**:

```
PROBLEM SCOPE:
  Affected users/stakeholders: [N]
  Frequency of impact: [X times/week or month]
  Time cost per instance: [Y hours]
  Total annual cost (time): N × frequency × Y hrs × $loaded_rate

  Error/rework cost: [Z errors/month × cost/error × 12]

  Risk exposure: [Probability × Impact]

TOTAL ANNUAL COST OF PROBLEM: $[Sum]

EXPECTED VALUE CAPTURED (conservative):
  Assumption: We capture [60-80%] of opportunity
  Conservative annual value: $[Amount]
  3-year value: $[Amount × 3, undiscounted]
```

## Step 3: Articulate the Cost of Inaction

This is the most underused tool in opportunity framing. Make doing nothing feel expensive.

### **Cost of Inaction Framework**:

```
SHORT-TERM (0-6 months):
  "If we don't act by Q2, we will [miss the budget cycle / lose the window / continue losing X per month]."

MEDIUM-TERM (6-18 months):
  "Competitors will [release this capability / capture this segment / erode our position]."
  "Technical debt will [make this harder / more expensive / blocked by other initiatives]."

LONG-TERM (18+ months):
  "The compounding cost of not solving this: [regulatory exposure / customer attrition / market share]."

OPPORTUNITY COST:
  "Every month we wait costs us [$/mo in known costs]."
```

### **Urgency Triggers to Identify**:
- Regulatory deadline
- Competitor move
- Contract/commercial renewal
- Budget cycle window
- Organizational change (leadership, restructure)
- Technology end-of-life
- Market event (launch, shift, disruption)

## Step 4: Frame Strategic Fit

Connect the opportunity to what leadership already cares about.

### **Strategic Alignment Map**:

```
COMPANY STRATEGY:    [Strategic pillar / goal]
        ↓
HOW THIS CONNECTS:   [Explicit link to that priority]
        ↓
CONTRIBUTION:        [What this initiative delivers toward that goal]

Example:
Company priority: "Reduce operational cost by 15% by FY27"
This initiative: Eliminates $180K in manual reconciliation labour
Contribution: Delivers 0.4% of the 15% cost reduction target
```

### **Strategic Framing Language**:

| Frame | When to use |
|-------|-------------|
| "Growth enabler" | Links to revenue or market expansion goals |
| "Efficiency multiplier" | Links to cost reduction or productivity goals |
| "Risk mitigator" | Links to compliance, resilience, or risk goals |
| "Capability builder" | Links to platform, talent, or transformation goals |
| "Customer outcome" | Links to NPS, retention, or customer success goals |

---

## Step 5: The "Why Now" Case

Every opportunity needs a window — a reason this decision can't wait.

```
WHY NOW TRIGGERS:

Market window:
"Our top competitor launched this capability last quarter. 
We have 6 months before this becomes a standard expectation."

Internal window:
"The architecture migration in Q3 creates a natural point to include 
this — retrofitting it post-migration costs 3× more."

Financial window:
"This initiative qualifies for the digital transformation budget 
which must be allocated by June 30."

Regulatory window:
"Compliance deadline is July 1. We need 4 months to implement, 
meaning kick-off must begin by March."
```

## The One-Page Opportunity Frame

Write this before building a full business case:

```
OPPORTUNITY: [Name]
DATE: [Date] | AUTHOR: [Name]

PROBLEM STATEMENT:
[One paragraph. Who, what, when, with what measurable impact.]

SCALE OF IMPACT:
Affected: [N users / processes / customers]
Annual cost: $[X] | Risk exposure: $[Y] | Revenue at risk: $[Z]

OPPORTUNITY SIZE:
Conservative case: $[Amount] annual value
Realistic case: $[Amount] annual value
3-year NPV (estimated): $[Amount]

COST OF INACTION:
[2-3 sentences on what happens if we don't act, and by when.]

STRATEGIC FIT:
Aligns to: [Company priority/pillar]
Contribution: [Specific metric/goal this advances]

WHY NOW:
[The trigger or window that makes this time-sensitive.]

PROPOSED RESPONSE:
[1 sentence on the initiative being proposed — not the solution detail.]

INVESTMENT RANGE (rough):
Est. cost: $[Range] | Timeframe: [Range] | Team: [Rough resourcing]

NEXT STEP:
[Specific ask — approval to proceed to business case / discovery / etc.]
```

## Common Framing Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Leading with the solution | Frame the problem first — always |
| Vague impact ("improve efficiency") | Quantify: hours, dollars, errors, risk |
| No urgency | Add the cost of waiting and the closing window |
| Too broad ("transform the business") | Scope to what this initiative specifically delivers |
| No strategic link | Explicitly name the company goal this advances |
| Buried ask | Make the ask explicit and early |
| One-sided (only benefits) | Acknowledge trade-offs — it builds credibility |
